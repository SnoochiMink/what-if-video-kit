# Volcano

## Field — buoyant plume plus a gravity current

Two distinct systems with opposite behaviour, and the contrast between them is the whole story.

**Eruption column** — hot, buoyant, rising fast and spreading at the tropopause into the
classic umbrella. Model as an upward velocity field with strong turbulent noise and radial
spreading above a ceiling height.

**Pyroclastic density current** — denser than air, so it *hugs the ground* and accelerates
downhill at 100–700 km/h. This is what kills. Model as an advancing front like a tsunami, but
compressible, following terrain and overtopping ridges.

```js
// column: buoyant above the vent, spreading at the ceiling
const r = Math.hypot(p.x - VENT.x, p.z - VENT.z);
const core = Math.exp(-r*r / (2 * FIELD.vent * FIELD.vent));
const rise = FIELD.v0 * core * (1 - smooth(FIELD.ceil * 0.8, FIELD.ceil, p.y));
const spread = smooth(FIELD.ceil * 0.85, FIELD.ceil, p.y) * FIELD.v0 * 0.4;

// PDC: a ground-hugging front that follows the terrain
const front = FIELD.pdcR;
const inFlow = smooth(front + 40, front - 60, Math.hypot(p.x-VENT.x, p.z-VENT.z));
const hug = Math.exp(-(p.y - terrainY(p.x, p.z)) / 35);
```

## Visual signature

The column is grey-brown and backlit, with visible convective rolls and lightning generated by
ash friction. The pyroclastic flow is darker, faster, and follows valleys. Ashfall accumulates
over time — progressively grey everything, weigh down and collapse roofs, and bury the ground.
Lava glows and self-illuminates; ash does not.

Ash accumulation on surfaces over the video's run is one of the most effective slow-burn
visuals in this whole genre. Drive a grey overlay from a per-surface accumulation value.

## Destruction order

| Agent | Effect |
|---|---|
| Ashfall 10 cm | roads impassable, visibility near zero |
| Ashfall 30 cm (wet) | roof collapse — wet ash is roughly twice as dense |
| Ballistics | incandescent bombs, impact craters, fires |
| Pyroclastic flow | total destruction; buildings sheared at ground level |
| Lahar | mudflow following river valleys, often days later |

Pyroclastic flows destroy by both heat and dynamic pressure. Buildings fail on the upstream
side first, and what remains is scoured.

## Camera beats

Cold-open inside the ash cloud. A normal town with the mountain on the horizon. The vent
opening. The column climbing, timelapse-fast. Ash beginning to fall like snow. The collapse of
the column and the flow starting downhill. Street level as it arrives. Everything grey and
buried.

## Audio

A huge low-frequency roar, mostly infrasound, so lean on the sub oscillator. Sharp cracks from
ballistics. Ashfall is eerily quiet — a soft hiss. Lightning in the plume. For the pyroclastic
flow, a rising roar plus the crackle of ignition. Use the quiet of ashfall as contrast before
the flow arrives.

## Verified facts

- The 1815 eruption of Mount Tambora in Indonesia cooled global temperatures enough that 1816
  became known as the "Year Without a Summer" across Europe and North America.
- The 1883 eruption of Krakatoa was heard roughly 4,800 km away on Rodrigues Island, one of the
  loudest sounds in recorded history.
- The 1980 eruption of Mount St. Helens produced a lateral blast that flattened about 600 km²
  of forest in minutes.
