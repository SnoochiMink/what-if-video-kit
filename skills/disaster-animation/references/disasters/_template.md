# <Disaster name>

Copy this file to add a disaster. Keep the headings — the engine layers assume them, and the
consistency is what lets you start a new scenario by swapping one file.

## Field

Which archetype from `forces.md` applies: vortex, blast wave, ground displacement, advancing
front, radial attraction, or a sum of two. Give the sampling expression and the keyframe tracks
that drive its state. Name the one real-world number you anchor the field to.

## Visual signature

What makes this disaster instantly recognisable in a still frame. Sky colour, the shape and
motion of the main phenomenon, what the light does, what accumulates on surfaces over time.

Be concrete about what distinguishes it from the lookalike disaster — a pyroclastic flow from a
dust storm, a tsunami from a flood, a hurricane from a tornado. Those distinctions are where
accuracy is visible.

## Destruction order

A table mapping an intensity measure to its effect, with the real thresholds. Then the
*sequence* in which a structure fails, including any gating — the rule that one thing cannot
fail until another already has. Sequence matters more than exact numbers.

## Camera beats

Seven or eight shots covering the 61 seconds, starting with the cold open on the climax. Say
what each shot is *for*, not just where the camera is.

## Audio

The continuous beds and their filter recipes, the one-shot events, and the one silence or
contrast that gives this disaster its emotional shape.

## Verified facts

Two or three real, checkable facts with date, place and number, for the end card. Prefer
specific and surprising over large and vague. Verify before shipping — one invented statistic
in the comments costs the account its credibility.
